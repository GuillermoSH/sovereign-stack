## Portainer, while it made sense

I didn’t start with **Dockge**. The original Compose manager was **Portainer**, and it stayed until the Gastromind presentation. Multi-user, live logs, stack management, system and container monitoring, volume views… At the time it was exactly what I needed to run several services for different people at once.

## Switching to Dockge

Right after delivering the project I switched to **Dockge**, looking for something simpler day to day — I no longer needed multi-user or a feature-heavy UI. One container, no separate database to maintain, and an interface that goes straight to what I actually use: edit Compose, bring stacks up and down, and little else.

## What I lost: live logs

That’s where I lost a piece I miss: **watching live logs from the UI itself**. Now I do that from a terminal over SSH:

```bash
docker compose logs -f <service>
```

Not as cozy as having it built in, but for how I use the homelab today, the rest of Dockge makes up for it.

## An alternative on my radar

If I ever miss that convenience more, there are projects meant to fill the gap without going back to a heavier manager: **Dozzle**, for example, is a real-time log viewer that runs as its own container without touching the rest of the stack. I haven’t set it up yet — I don’t miss logs enough to add another service — but it’s the option I’ve noted if I change my mind.

## And Dockge — for how much longer?

Even so, it already has an expiry date: in Reddit homelab threads people say maintenance is winding down. I haven’t picked a replacement yet, so when the time comes I’ll look at it calmly.
